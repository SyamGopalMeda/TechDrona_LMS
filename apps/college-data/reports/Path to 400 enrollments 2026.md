# Concentrate on 30 colleges and December to reach 400

Tech Drona can reach 400 confirmed paid enrollments by 31 December 2026 only if it puts its effort into about 30 large colleges and plans for most confirmations to land between 28 November and 22 December. Spreading thinly across the 107 colleges in its database will not get there. The database holds about **80,055 eligible final-year students (estimated)** in relevant IT, IT-allied and circuit branches, so 400 is **0.50% of the full pool and 0.91% of a focus-30 set** of 43,974 students ([capacity notes](file:///C:/Users/SYAM/Downloads/colleges_data-main/colleges_data-main/research_notes/Path%20to%20400%20enrollments%202026/capacity_and_allocation.md)). Pool size is not what limits the plan. The limits are reach, conversion and the calendar. The best-evidenced exam research shows the JNTUK-affiliated majority sitting Mid-II on **9–14 November** and end-semester theory on **16–27 November**, with lab externals pushed to unspecified dates after that ([exam status report](file:///C:/Users/SYAM/Downloads/colleges_data-main/colleges_data-main/BTech%20exam%20status%203-1%204-1%20Nov%202026.md)). That leaves about five open weeks for most final-years. The team has **no history at all**: the enrollments, people, targets and activities tables are empty. Every conversion rate and staffing ratio below is therefore a labelled scenario, not a forecast. The plan needs 30 college leads and roughly 100–170 student ambassadors. Workshops approved by the TPO or HOD should be the main acquisition channel, with WhatsApp used only for opt-in follow-up. The portal should count only payment-confirmed enrollments, net of refunds, keyed on one record per student. Three findings should change the allocation before outreach starts. GGU has no final-year B.Tech batch. VFSTR's database intake may be overstated. And 11 of the focus-30 colleges, including Aditya, Chalapathi University, GGU and VIT-AP, have no verified TPO contact.

## The pool is large, estimated and concentrated in Guntur

Of 170 colleges in the database, 113 are operational. The 107 that carry numbered relevant branches hold 80,055 eligible 4-1 students, but **only 4,920 (6.1%) rest on verified intake**: KL, SAHE, Amrita Amaravati and NIT AP. The other 93.9% are derived mostly from 2026-27 CAP convener seats used as a proxy for the 2023 batch. Attrition, lateral entry and unfilled seats are not modelled, so the true pool is probably smaller ([capacity notes](file:///C:/Users/SYAM/Downloads/colleges_data-main/colleges_data-main/research_notes/Path%20to%20400%20enrollments%202026/capacity_and_allocation.md)). Guntur district alone holds **26,184 eligible (32.7%)**, followed by Kakinada (10,206), NTR (9,765) and West Godavari (7,854). The top 30 colleges hold 55.9% of the pool. By department family, CSE (28,485) and AI/AI-ML (21,420) together make up 62%.

How many colleges must be activated depends entirely on an assumed conversion rate, because the team has measured none. At 1% of eligible students, the 25 largest colleges suffice. At 2%, eight do. At 0.5%, all 107 are needed and there is no margin left ([capacity notes](file:///C:/Users/SYAM/Downloads/colleges_data-main/colleges_data-main/research_notes/Path%20to%20400%20enrollments%202026/capacity_and_allocation.md)). The only large Indian benchmark is Meritto's 2026 index of 4.3 crore inquiries. It reports **1.06% lead-to-enrollment overall, 0.74% for first-time inquirers and 3.00% for re-inquired leads**, and same-day responses convert at twice the rate of one-week responses. These figures mostly describe institutional admissions, not short courses ([The Wire/PTI](https://m.thewire.in/article/ptiprnews/ai-led-discovery-converts-6x-better-than-digital-reveals-merittos-enrollment-index-2026)). Workshop and webinar figures come only from vendors and blogs: 3–8% from workshop to sale ([inqrise](https://inqrise.in/blog/online-coaching-platform-marketing-india/)), and 30–40% registrant show-up with 5–12% of attendees buying ([acceleroi](https://www.acceleroi.com/blog/unlocking-success-exploring-the-average-conversion-rate-for-online-courses)). No source anywhere covers cold calls to a TPO or HOD that turn into workshop slots.

Applying those ranges to the target, purely as arithmetic, gives the required student volumes:

| Scenario (label: assumption) | Rate | Volume needed for 400 |
|---|---|---|
| Cold or first-time leads | ~1% | ~40,000 leads |
| Portal's configured `min_conversion` (enrolled ÷ reached) | 5% | 8,000 reached |
| Workshop registrants to paid | 3–8% | ~5,000–13,300 registrants |
| Live attendees to paid | 5–12% | ~3,300–8,000 attendees |

The practical meaning is that Tech Drona should plan to put roughly **5,000–8,000 students into the room or onto a live session** across the focus colleges. That is about 11–18% of the focus-30 pool. It should never rely on cold lists.

## The exam calendar: the CoE-signed revision beats the proposed calendar

The benchmarks note gives a JNTUK 3-1/4-1 calendar of II Mid on **2–7 November** and end exams on **16–28 November**, with 4-2 internship starting 30 November. It takes these from "proposed" calendars on an unofficial mirror ([jntufastupdates 4-1 calendar](https://www.jntufastupdates.com/jntuk-4-1-4-2-academic-calendar-2026-27/)). The project's own exam research is better evidenced and supersedes it. JNTUK's revised CoE notice of 29 October 2026 moved R23 Mid-II to **9–13 November (3-1) and 9–14 November (4-1)**. It set end-sem theory on alternate days, **16–27 November for 4-1 and 17–26 November for 3-1**, and postponed lab externals until after the theory exams with no dates given. These documents carry the CoE signature, reference number and date. Two independent copies of the end-sem timetable agree, and a separate verification pass re-opened them ([4-1 end-sem PDF mirror](https://files.jntufastupdates.com/download/iv-i-b-tech-r23-exams-november-2026/); [4-1 Mid-II PDF mirror](https://files.jntufastupdates.com/download/4-1_r23-ii_mid-exams-november-2026/); [exam status report](file:///C:/Users/SYAM/Downloads/colleges_data-main/colleges_data-main/BTech%20exam%20status%203-1%204-1%20Nov%202026.md)). Both sources still rely on mirrors, because jntuk.edu.in posts no exam PDFs. The R20 supplementary also keeps exam centres busy until 7 December, and the cyclone-postponed supplementaries have no new dates yet.

In practice, 4–8 November is a short open window. **9–27 November is blocked** for the JNTUK majority, peaking on 17 November, when 151 college-semester rows are in an end-sem window. 28 November to 31 December is open, but undated lab externals will intrude on it. The 4-2 internship start of 30 November comes only from the unofficial calendar. If it holds, final-years will be harder to reach on campus but will feel more placement urgency, which suits online cohorts. In the weeks when 44.9% of the pool is inside an end-sem window, the team's work is setup: TPO calls, appointing leads and ambassadors, and building opt-in interest lists. Confirmations should not be expected in those weeks.

## Leads, ambassadors and channels: who delivers what

Each focus college needs **one college lead**, 30 in total. The lead owns the institutional relationship and one target figure. That means securing TPO or principal permission and at least one workshop slot timed to the college's open window, and keeping the college's exam dates current in the portal. The lead also appoints and supervises the college's ambassadors. The capacity analysis gives ambassador scenarios: **167 at one per branch with a target** (2.4 enrollments each) or **103 at one per 500 eligible students** (3.9 each). For the full 107-college plan the figure is 293 ([capacity notes](file:///C:/Users/SYAM/Downloads/colleges_data-main/colleges_data-main/research_notes/Path%20to%20400%20enrollments%202026/capacity_and_allocation.md)). No programme publishes productivity per ambassador. Internshala admits some ambassadors are "inactive from day 1" ([Internshala blog](https://internshala.com/blog/?p=15482)), and IMAV requires **three confirmed enrollments a month to stay active**, paying ₹1,000 for each confirmed, non-cancelled enrollment ([Jobaaj](https://www.jobaaj.com/job/imav-learning-academy-campus-ambassador-commission-based-india-0-to-1-years-1245242)). The benchmarks note infers, without a source, that perhaps half of ambassadors produce nothing. Recruiting toward the upper scenario of about 167, then pruning, is therefore safer than recruiting 103. Each ambassador's deliverables are:

- registrations collected with consent
- attendance at their department's session
- a personal target of 2–4 confirmed enrollments

Pay only on confirmed, non-refunded payment, within the observed market of **₹150–₹1,000 per enrollment** ([origennt](https://origennt.com/campus-ambassador.html); [Career Craft](https://careercraftinstitute.in/campus-ambassador/)). Add a fixed fee for organising a session, as Internshala does.

No source ranks channels numerically for Indian engineering colleges. The indirect evidence points one way. Aggregator leads bring volume but few enrollments. Re-engaged leads convert four times better than first-timers. Fast follow-up doubles conversion ([The Wire/PTI](https://m.thewire.in/article/ptiprnews/ai-led-discovery-converts-6x-better-than-digital-reveals-merittos-enrollment-index-2026)). Large players such as Testbook sign colleges through institutional agreements ([referralflo](https://referralflo.ai/campus-ambassador-program)). The channel order should therefore be:

1. TPO- or HOD-approved in-college or online workshops with recorded attendance
2. referrals from paid students and ambassadors
3. department WhatsApp groups seeded only through those workshops
4. Instagram and YouTube for awareness and retargeting
5. no bought or scraped lists

On price, students report paying about ₹3,000–₹5,000 for short skill courses, and free NPTEL anchors the low end ([Careers360](https://news.careers360.com/btech-courses-civil-mechanical-cse-it-engineering-students-pay-online-skill-education-syllabus-faculty-aicte-jobs-industry-gap-nep)). Exam-prep products convert better because of deadline pressure ([productgrowth.in](https://productgrowth.in/insights/edtech/free-to-paid/)), so placement-season urgency is the natural message after 28 November.

## Operating rules for the portal's calling and funnel system

**Call outcomes and contact validity.** The portal should hold contact validity as a property of each phone number (Working, Non-working or Unverified), separate from the outcome of any single call. Outcome codes should be mandatory, kept to about 10–15, and each code should map to exactly one next action. This follows LeadSquared's practice of mandatory post-call actions and vendor guidance on keeping code lists short ([LeadSquared](https://help.leadsquared.com/?p=6889); [Prospeo](https://prospeo.io/s/what-is-call-disposition)). HubSpot's default outcomes need Indian additions such as "switched off" and "not reachable" ([HubSpot](https://developers.hubspot.com/docs/api/crm/calls)).

| Outcome | Validity | Next action |
|---|---|---|
| Connected – interested (right person) | Working | Institutional stage → Interested; send material same day; next step within 2 working days |
| Connected – callback (date/time) | Working | Task at the requested slot (±30 min) |
| Connected – not decision-maker / referred | Working (wrong role) | Create the referred contact |
| Connected – not interested (reason) | Working | Close 60–90 days |
| Connected – do not call | Working | Permanent suppression |
| No answer / busy / switched off / not reachable | Unverified (busy = probably working) | Retry per cadence, different slot |
| Left message with office | Working (office line) | Retry in 1–2 days |
| Wrong number / invalid / out of service | Non-working | Mark invalid; source an alternative |

**Cadence.** Make two attempts on day 1, at 10:00–11:30 and 15:00–16:30, then call again on days 2, 4, 7 and 10, rotating time slots. Send a WhatsApp or email touch after attempts 2 and 4. After six no-connects, mark the number "unreachable" and move to another number. Mark a college unreachable only when every known number is exhausted. This draws on research where leads first reached after seven or more attempts converted 45% worse ([CustomerThink](https://customerthink.com/?p=50152)) and on timing studies that put mid-morning and late afternoon best and lunch worst ([JustCall compilation](https://justcall.io/blog/auto-dialer-benchmarks-2026.html)). None of this evidence is specific to Indian college offices, so the team should log connect rate by hour for its first two weeks and then adjust. A first pass over 30 colleges at about 15 dials each is roughly 450 dials. At the 44–49 manual dials a day reported for US SDRs ([Revenue.io](https://www.revenue.io/blog/why-sales-call-connection-rates-are-dropping)), that is about 9–10 caller-days.

**Priority score.** Score each college account as (Fit 0–50 + Intent 0–30) × Reachability. Reachability is 0 with no working number, 0.5 with only an office line and 1 with a verified TPO or HOD mobile. Call tier A daily, tier B per cadence and tier C monthly. With so few accounts and no history, a transparent points table beats predictive scoring ([Pedowitz](https://www.pedowitzgroup.com/how-do-you-define-fit-vs.-engagement-scoring)).

**Funnel stages.** Keep the institutional funnel and the student funnel separate, and never sum them. The college stages are Not contacted, Contacted, Interested, Meeting, then Partnered/Active. The student funnel uses observable events:

| Stage | Definition (event) | Counts toward 400? |
|---|---|---|
| Lead | Student record with a valid, consented mobile | No |
| Interested | Positive response on call, WhatsApp or at a session | No |
| Registered | Account or registration form created (with consent notice) | No |
| Applied | Course application or seat request submitted | No |
| Confirmed | Payment received and not refunded | **Yes, only this** |
| Dropped/Refunded | Terminal; subtracted from Confirmed, never deleted | Subtracts |

**Deduplication.** Each student gets one record, keyed on a normalised 10-digit mobile (strip +91, a leading 0 and spaces), with email as a secondary key. The stage field records only the highest stage reached, with a date stamp for each stage. Counts are "stage reached by date", not stage-entry events. Attribution to a college and ambassador sits in its own field, and first verified referral wins. A leaderboard credit never adds to the total. This mirrors LeadSquared's unique-phone and duplicate-merge rules ([LeadSquared](https://help.leadsquared.com/avoid-creating-duplicate-leads/)). Shared group numbers (KIET, GGU/GIET, SRK/VITW) should link to one account.

**Dashboard and projection.** The dashboard should show five groups of measures:

- **Activity:** dials, connects and conversations per caller per day.
- **Connect rate:** both per dial and per contact after N attempts. The two differ by more than two times: Belkins reports 9.9% per dial against 24.5% per prospect.
- **Data quality:** contact validity (working ÷ attempted numbers) and the percentage of focus colleges with a verified decision-maker.
- **Funnel counts:** colleges per stage, and students by highest stage reached, with conversion between adjacent stages.
- **Pace:** a burn-up chart of net confirmed enrollments against the straight line to 400.

For projection, the required rate is (400 − net confirmed) ÷ weeks left. The projected total is net confirmed + trailing 2–4-week rate × weeks left, with best and worst cones drawn from the minimum and maximum weekly rates ([ScrumDesk](https://flowanalyzer.scrumdesk.com/rules/BC-05); [Atlassian](https://www.atlassian.com/blog/archives/burnup-reports-agile-teams-guest-blog/amp)). Until two weeks of confirmations exist, the projection should run on leading indicators instead: slots booked, attendees and registrations. The portal's existing `low_outreach` rule (reached < 3 × target × elapsed fraction) still works as a floor ([capacity notes](file:///C:/Users/SYAM/Downloads/colleges_data-main/colleges_data-main/research_notes/Path%20to%20400%20enrollments%202026/capacity_and_allocation.md)).

## Compliance: low-cost discipline now avoids SIM-level penalties

India's DPDP Rules were notified on 14 November 2025 with an **18-month phased compliance window** ([PIB backgrounder](https://static.pib.gov.in/WriteReadData/specificdocs/documents/2025/nov/doc20251117695301.pdf)). Notice, consent and children's-data rules commence later, with secondary sources placing them around May 2027 ([AZB & Partners](https://www.azbpartners.com/bank/update-indias-digital-personal-data-protection-framework-comes-into-effect/)). Most obligations are not yet enforceable, but the direction is fixed:

- a purpose-specific consent notice
- consent that can be revoked at any time
- verifiable parental consent for anyone under 18
- penalties of up to ₹200 crore for children's-data breaches

The portal should therefore record consent and its source on every student record, keep a suppression list fed by "do not call" outcomes and opt-outs, block contact until consent exists, and capture age, routing minors (some first-years) to a parental-consent path. It should never ingest bought, scraped or college-shared rosters without the students' own opt-in. On telemarketing, TRAI's February 2025 TCCCPR amendments move promotional calls to the 140 series and restrict telemarketing from ordinary 10-digit numbers. Repeat violations bar a sender's outgoing services for 15 days, and further violations bring disconnection across all providers for a year, triggerable on 5 complaints in 10 days ([Saraf & Partners](https://sarafpartners.com/?p=31162)). TRAI's July 2026 clarification makes the 140 series exclusive to promotional calls and requires registration to use it ([SCC Online](https://www.scconline.com/blog/post/2026/07/18/trai-clarifies-1600-and-140-number-series-to-prevent-misinformation)). One-to-one relationship calls to college officials are low risk. Bulk promotional calls to students from personal mobiles are the highest-risk practice. Whether B2B calls to officials count as "commercial communication", and what calling-hour limits apply, were not confirmed, so the team should take legal advice before scaling student calling.

## Risks and data gaps

Four gaps could each move the result by tens of enrollments:

- **Estimated pool.** The pool estimate rests on proxies for 93.9% of students.
- **Unknown exam dates.** 36 of the 107 colleges, holding 46.5% of the pool, have no 4-1 window. JNTUK lab externals are undated.
- **Missing intake.** SRM AP and VVIT University, both large Amaravati-belt universities, are counted at zero because their intake is pending.
- **No conversion history.** No conversion or ambassador-productivity figure exists. All rates here come from vendors, blogs or one admissions-focused index.

Other risks are documented but unquantified:

- the cyclone disruption that closed Guntur-area colleges on 3 November
- MPTC/ZPTC election closures
- final-years possibly leaving campus for 4-2 internships from 30 November
- TPO gatekeeping, which typically comes with an expectation of a free value-add
- a December holiday dip that the schedule does not model

The highest-value fixes, in order, are verified 2023-batch strength for the top 10 colleges, 4-1 dates for KL, Chalapathi, VIT-AP, VFSTR, KHIT and Pragati, and intake for SRM AP and VVIT University ([capacity notes](file:///C:/Users/SYAM/Downloads/colleges_data-main/colleges_data-main/research_notes/Path%20to%20400%20enrollments%202026/capacity_and_allocation.md)).

## Conclusion

The arithmetic says 400 is easy: under 1% of the focus pool. The calendar and the empty history say it is hard. Every week from now to 27 November is worth more as setup than as selling. The first two weeks of call outcomes, connect rates and workshop yields are the only real data the team will ever have for re-planning, so the portal's first job is to measure those rates cleanly, not to show a target line.

The largest single lever is fixing the top of the list before calling. GGU's target points at students who do not exist. VFSTR's may be inflated. Four of the six largest focus colleges have no verified TPO. Correcting those three things, and confirming the exam dates of the 13 top-30 colleges with none, changes where perhaps a third of the 400 comes from. The cost is a few days of calls that the team has to make anyway.
