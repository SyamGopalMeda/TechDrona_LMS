# Coastal Andhra colleges hide most placement data

Only **48 of the 128 active B.Tech institutions** in Guntur, Krishna (Vijayawada), East Godavari and West Godavari publish any usable placement figure for the 2024, 2025 or 2026 graduating batches. Among those that do, the typical B.Tech graduate is placed at a **median of about Rs 4.0–4.5 LPA**, and roughly **two in three graduates are placed** in the year they finish. Pooled across the 19 colleges with graduate and placed counts for both years, that was **65.5% for batch 2024 (11,994 of 18,306) and 67.8% for batch 2025 (12,897 of 19,019)**. Pay sorts the field more cleanly than placement rate. Only five institutions, **KL University (8.5–8.7 LPA), NIT Andhra Pradesh (7.6 → 6.4), SRM AP (7.0), Vignan's Foundation (6.8–7.0) and JNTUK UCE Kakinada (6.0 → 5.0)**, report UG medians clearly above 5 LPA. The best-documented placement-rate leader is **PVPSIT in Vijayawada (88.0% → 96.2%, median 4.5 LPA, from its own NIRF PDF)**. Every number here comes second-hand or from college-hosted files, because nirfindia.org blocked all scripted access. Three of the four regions rely mostly on Careers360 tables that reproduce NIRF data. Batch 2026 is still mid-drive: only 8 institutions have partial figures, all drive or offer data that cannot be compared, and final numbers will not appear until the NIRF 2027 submissions. The companion file `reports/btech_placements_2024_2026.csv` holds all 384 institution-year rows (128 × 3), with status, confidence, source and note on each row.

## Four in ten institutions disclose anything, and the gap tracks tier

Disclosure falls off steeply below the top tier. **All 8 Tier 1 institutions, 28 of 32 Tier 2 colleges and only 12 of 88 Tier 3 colleges** have at least one usable figure. The 80 institutions with nothing for any year are almost all small affiliated or newly autonomous colleges that do not take part in NIRF. Researchers in every region reached the same dead end: no Careers360 NIRF table, a 404 placement page, or nothing but AICTE branch percentages computed on first-year intake. Four Tier 2 colleges also have no data: RV Institute of Technology, Malineni Lakshmaiah Women's, Pydah and BIET. The NIRF-style rows that do exist come from two places. One is Careers360's college pages and its [AP EAMCET 2026 college-wise placement article](https://engineering.careers360.com/articles/ap-eamcet-2026-college-wise-placement-statistics-past-year-analysis), whose year columns run one year behind the graduating batch (its "2024" column matches KL's 2024-25 table). The other is the NIRF data-capture PDFs that some colleges host on their own sites.

## Batch 2024: Tier 1 universities lead on pay, mid-sized autonomous colleges on placement rate

Ranked by UG median salary, batch 2024 (AY 2023-24, reported in NIRF 2025) is led by **KL University at 8.52 LPA**, with 1,854 of 2,674 graduates placed ([Careers360 KL](https://www.careers360.com/university/koneru-lakshmaiah-education-foundation-guntur/placement)). ## Batch 2025: rates edge up while medians drift down

Batch 2025 (AY 2024-25, reported in NIRF 2026) shows a split trend. Among the 19 colleges with counts in both years, **placement rates rose at 13 and fell at 6**, and the pooled rate moved from 65.5% to 67.8%. Medians moved the other way. Of 31 institutions with a median in both years, **12 fell, 8 were flat and 11 rose**, and the median of all reported medians slipped from 4.2 to 4.0 LPA. The declines cluster in the better-paid, IT-heavy colleges. NIT AP fell from 7.56 to 6.41 LPA, a drop Careers360 itself highlights ([Careers360 NIT AP](https://www.careers360.com/university/national-institute-of-technology-andhra-pradesh/placement)). SRKR fell from 4.50 to 3.50 ([Careers360 SRKR](https://www.careers360.com/colleges/sagi-ramakrishnam-raju-engineering-college-bhimavaram/placement)), and VVIT, Pragati, Swarnandhra and UCEK each lost at least a lakh. This fits colleges placing more students into lower-paying mass-recruiter roles in a weaker IT hiring cycle, though the notes cannot confirm the causal story. **KL University (8.68 LPA)** is the exception at the top, rising slightly while keeping 71.6% placed.

A clear pattern runs across tiers in both years. **Tier 1 institutions have the highest medians (median of medians 7.0 LPA in 2024, 6.4 in 2025) but not the highest placement rates (about 60–64%).** The Tier 3 colleges that disclose anything report the highest rates (about 79–80%) at the lowest pay (3.0–3.25 LPA). Two forces likely drive this. Tier 1 graduates opt out into higher studies, and only the stronger Tier 3 colleges publish at all, a survivorship effect. Placement rate alone is therefore a poor guide to college quality in this region. It is best read alongside the median and the share of graduates going to higher studies.

## How to read these numbers: six caveats that change rankings

**NIRF's own portal was unreachable.** nirfindia.org returned HTTP 403 to every scripted and WebFetch request in all four regional searches. Its ranking pages and institutional PDFs were therefore never read directly. The confidence column separates three kinds of source. "NIRF (college-hosted PDF)" means the data-capture PDF a college posts on its own domain, which is the same file it submits to NIRF. "Aggregator citing NIRF" means a Careers360 or Shiksha table that says it reproduces NIRF data. "Aggregator" and "College site" mean drive figures with no NIRF lineage. Only **13 of the 91 data-bearing institution-years** (8 institutions) rest on college-hosted NIRF PDFs. The 27 institutions with "Aggregator citing NIRF" values should be rechecked in a browser against the official PDFs. Several Careers360 pages mislabel years or print placement % figures that their own counts do not support, including Pragati, GGU, VITB, SRKR, Ramachandra, Sasi and NRI Agiripalli. The VITB and SVECW 2024 medians, for instance, are an identical Rs 4.35 L and may be cross-copied.

**Placement % uses one strict definition:** students placed ÷ UG graduates in stipulated time, computed to one decimal wherever both counts exist. Careers360's published percentages use placed ÷ (graduates − higher studies). The Guntur researcher confirmed this exactly on ten colleges, for example RVR 709/(1,057−97) = 73.85%. Other sources count higher-studies students as placed (PVPSIT's 98.9%) or divide by first-year AICTE intake. The strict definition lowers figures for colleges that send many graduates to higher studies, such as Vignan's Foundation, VVIT and NEC. Where only a reported percentage existed (Amrita Sai, Usha Rama, DNR, VRSEC 2026), the CSV shows it with a note, and the rankings exclude it.

**Offers are not students.** Drive tallies such as KL's 7,050 offers, Aditya's 3,450, LBRCE's 1,149 and RVR's 1,132 count multiple offers per student and often include PG programmes. These appear in the Note column and never in "Students placed".

**Highest packages are one-off claims.** Every headline figure flagged "⚑ one-off claim" in the CSV is a single offer, often international, group-wide or of disputed year. Examples include VIT-AP's "Rs 1 crore" (possibly the VIT Vellore group figure), Amrita's university-wide 80.4 LPA, KL's 75, SRM AP's 53, NIT AP's 44.1 (student reviews put the top at 12–16 LPA), KITS Akshar's 51.36 banner and Pragati's 40. These say nothing about a typical outcome and should not be used to rank colleges.

**2026 is partial and ongoing**, as described above. It is labelled "Partial (ongoing)" in the CSV and is excluded from every ranking.

**Absence of data is not evidence of poor placement.** The 80 no-data institutions are overwhelmingly non-NIRF Tier 3 colleges. Their outcomes are unknown, not zero.

## Conclusion

Placement statistics in coastal Andhra separate cleanly on only one axis. The handful of Tier 1 universities pay medians of 6.4–8.7 LPA, and almost everyone else sits in a 3–5 LPA band where tier, NAAC grade and region barely move the median. Within that band, the real differentiator is how many graduates get placed: PVPSIT, VRSEC, Bapatla, Pragati and Vignan's Lara placed 83–96% in 2025, while MIC, Dhanekula and NRI Guntur place 29–53%. For families choosing a mid-tier college, a placement rate backed by a college-hosted NIRF PDF is worth more than any headline package. The 2024→2025 data also hints that higher rates came at the cost of lower medians in IT-heavy colleges.

The larger finding is structural. Roughly three-fifths of the region's engineering colleges publish nothing a prospective student can verify, and even the published figures need forensic reading to reconcile denominators, year labels and offers-versus-students. Three steps would turn this report from provisional into authoritative. First, open the NIRF 2025 and 2026 PDFs in a browser for the 27 "Aggregator citing NIRF" institutions. Second, OCR LBRCE's scanned 2026 file. Third, re-pull batch 2026 when the NIRF 2027 data capture lands in early 2027.
