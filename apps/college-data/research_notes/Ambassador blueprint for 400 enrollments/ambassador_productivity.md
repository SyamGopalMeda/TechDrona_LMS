# Campus ambassador productivity and economics: sizing the network for 400 paid enrollments (AP B.Tech, 3 Nov to 31 Dec 2026)

Research date: 3 Nov 2026. This builds on, and does not repeat, `research_notes/Path to 400 enrollments 2026/benchmarks_channels.md` section 2. That section covers Internshala ISP 2017, IMAV, ORIGENNT, Career Craft, Collegechalo, GfG and referralflo.

Labels:
- **[SOURCED]** has a URL.
- **[INFERRED]** is my reasoning or assumption, not a published number.

Source-quality tags:
- [OPERATOR]: the programme's own page.
- [JOB-LISTING]: an aggregator job post, unverified.
- [VENDOR]: marketing or affiliate software vendor, often US or non-India.
- [BLOG].

**Bottom line up front:** No Indian ed-tech publishes its paid enrollments per ambassador, its active rate, or its attrition. Every productivity number below comes from one of three things:
- non-India affiliate or campus-marketing vendors (activity rates, concentration of results, attrition)
- Indian operators' incentive rules, such as minimum-activity thresholds, which reveal what operators expect
- explicit assumptions

## 1. What Indian programmes disclose about activity, sign-ups per ambassador and top-performer distribution

### Takeaway
Indian programmes publish rewards, not results. The only India-specific signals of productivity are indirect:
- Internshala admits some ambassadors are "inactive from day 1".
- Its grand-prize bar historically required only **5 training sales in an edition**.
- A small academy requires **3 paid enrollments a month to stay active**.

Global affiliate benchmarks show a strong power law: the top 5% of partners produce 40-60% of revenue, and roughly half of partners are dormant.

**Global benchmarks on activity and concentration (non-India, affiliate)** [SOURCED, VENDOR] — [Track360](https://track360.io/learn/affiliate-program-performance-benchmarking/partner-engagement-and-retention)
- Share of registered partners active in the last 30 days: **25-40% is "healthy"**, below 15% signals a problem, and above 50% is typical of small curated programmes.
- 30-day activation: 30-45% average and 50%+ strong. 90-day activation: 40-55% average.
- **Directly recruited partners activate at 50-70%, against 15-25% for open sign-up forms.**
- Revenue concentration:

| Partner tier | Share of partners | Share of revenue |
|---|---|---|
| Top | 5% | 40-60% |
| Core | next 15-20% | 25-35% |
| Casual | next 25-30% | 10-15% |
| Long-tail and dormant | remaining 45-55% | under 5% |

- Median time to first conversion: 10-20 days is average and under 10 days is strong.

**US campus field-marketing cases** [SOURCED, VENDOR, US, illustrative scenarios not studies] — [Makai](https://www.makaiinc.com/resources/why-student-ambassador-programs-fail-a-field-diagnosis-and-recovery-guide)
- Only **4 of 30** reps had submitted event logs.
- Fewer than **20%** of 300+ applicants completed scheduled shifts.
- Reporting compliance fell **from 85% in September to 20% by early November, after midterms**.

### Inferences
- **[INFERRED]** Internshala's "5 training sales" grand-prize bar, combined with IMAV's "3 per month to stay active", suggests Indian operators expect a typical active ambassador to make **low single digits of paid sales per month**. Double digits come only from a small top tier.
- **[INFERRED]** Applying the Track360 tier shape to a student programme gives this planning distribution for recruited ambassadors over an 8-week sprint. It is an assumption, not a measurement:

| Segment | Share of recruited | Paid enrollments each (8 wk) | Contribution per 100 recruited |
|---|---|---|---|
| Dormant (0 sales) | 45-55% (use 50%) | 0 | 0 |
| Casual | 25-30% (use 28%) | 1-2 (use 1.5) | 42 |
| Core | 15-20% (use 17%) | 4-8 (use 6) | 102 |
| Stars | ~5% | 15-30 (use 20) | 100 |
| **Total** | 100% | **~2.4 per recruited, ~5 per active** | **~244** |

  - The top ~22% would produce about 83% of sales, which fits a typical 80/20 pattern.
- **[INFERRED]** The September-to-November collapse in the Makai scenario mirrors exactly the AP exam window (16 Nov-5 Dec, per benchmarks_channels.md section 4). Expect ambassador output near zero in those weeks.

### Gaps
- No Indian ed-tech publishes active rate, sales per ambassador, or the leaderboard distribution. Tech Drona must measure these itself from week 1.
- I could not find Unstop, Scaler, Newton, upGrad or Great Learning ambassador T&Cs with numbers.

## 2. Inactivity, drop-off and recruitment buffer

### Takeaway
There is no Indian figure. Non-India benchmarks imply that **45-75% of recruited ambassadors produce little or nothing**:
- 25-40% active in a 30-day period in affiliate programmes
- about half dormant
- 4 of 30 reporting in one campus case

Field-marketing vendors recommend over-recruiting by **30-40%** just for seasonal attrition among people who are already working. For commission-only student sellers the effective multiplier is about **2-2.5x** the number of active ambassadors needed.

### Cited Findings
- Active partner ratio is 25-40% in healthy affiliate programmes. Direct-recruited partners activate at 50-70%, open sign-ups at 15-25%. [SOURCED, VENDOR] — [Track360](https://track360.io/learn/affiliate-program-performance-benchmarking/partner-engagement-and-retention)
- Makai capacity guide (US paid-shift brand ambassadors) [SOURCED, VENDOR] — [Makai capacity guide](https://www.makaiinc.com/resources/how-to-plan-student-ambassador-program-capacity-and-coverage):
  - **seasonal attrition of 20-35%**
  - recruit **130-140% of baseline capacity**
  - **1 on-call reserve per 4 scheduled positions**, or a 25% reserve
  - a midterm scenario where "half the roster was lost"
- Flag any ambassador with **no verified activity for 2 consecutive weeks**. [SOURCED, VENDOR] — [Makai failure guide](https://www.makaiinc.com/resources/why-student-ambassador-programs-fail-a-field-diagnosis-and-recovery-guide)
- A vendor headline claims "90% of ambassador programs fail to activate members". The derivation is not shown, so treat it as marketing. [SOURCED, VENDOR, low reliability] — [NextBee](https://blog.nextbee.com/2025/12/26/why-90-of-ambassador-programs-fail-to-activate-members-2025-deep-dive-fixes/)
- Internshala: "some are inactive from day 1". [SOURCED, OPERATOR 2017] — [Internshala blog](https://internshala.com/blog/?p=15482)

### Inferences
- **[INFERRED]** Makai's 30-40% buffer covers attrition among *paid-shift* reps. Commission-only Indian student ambassadors behave more like affiliates, so use the affiliate active ratio:
  - With hand-picked, interviewed ambassadors and a college lead, assume **~50% active** (the direct-recruited 50-70% band, discounted for the exam period).
  - With an open Google-form sign-up, assume **25-35% active**.
- **[INFERRED] Buffer multiplier = 1 / expected active share.**

| Recruitment mode | Expected active share | Multiplier |
|---|---|---|
| Curated (interviewed, referred by TPO/HOD or a paid student) | ~50% | **2.0x** |
| Mixed | ~40% | **2.5x** |
| Open form | ~30% | **3.3x** |

- **[INFERRED]** Add a rolling replacement pool. Re-recruit to backfill anyone flagged inactive at the 2-week check (around 17 Nov). The week-1 to week-2 drop is the biggest one.

### Gaps
- There is no India-specific attrition curve by week. No data shows how commission-only vs stipend-plus-commission structures change inactivity.

## 3. Referral lead to paid conversion for ₹999-₹15,000 courses

### Takeaway
There is no Indian benchmark specific to peer referrals. The best proxies show warm or referred leads converting at **~2-4x cold leads**:
- Meritto: re-inquired leads 3.00% vs first-time 0.74%
- Extole (via aggregator): referral 3.6% vs 1.5% for site visitors

A peer-referred *conversation* with an interested classmate plausibly converts at 5-15% for a sub-₹5k course. That figure is inferred.

### Cited Findings
- Meritto Enrollment Index 2026 (already in benchmarks_channels.md): re-inquired leads 3.00% vs first-time 0.74%. Same-day response converts 2x better. [SOURCED, VENDOR, large sample] — [The Wire/PTI](https://m.thewire.in/article/ptiprnews/ai-led-discovery-converts-6x-better-than-digital-reveals-merittos-enrollment-index-2026)
- Referral to purchase is **3.6% vs 1.5%** for standard website visitors (attributed to Extole). Claims of "30% higher" or "3-5x" conversion are widely repeated but untraceable. [SOURCED, AGGREGATOR, attribution unreliable] — [Shno referral stats](https://www.shno.co/marketing-statistics/referral-marketing-statistics); [Opensend](https://www.opensend.com/post/referral-rate-statistics)
- Wharton-linked German bank study (about 10,000 customers): referred customers are worth at least **16% more** and churn about **18% less**. This is not student or India data. [SOURCED via aggregator] — [GrowSurf](https://growsurf.com/statistics/customer-referral-statistics/)
- Free-to-paid conversion is 2-5% for general learning and 8-15% where there is deadline pressure. [SOURCED, BLOG] — [productgrowth.in](https://productgrowth.in/insights/edtech/free-to-paid/)
- Observed prices B.Tech students pay for short courses: ₹3,000-₹5,000. [SOURCED, in benchmarks_channels.md section 5] — [Careers360](https://news.careers360.com/btech-courses-civil-mechanical-cse-it-engineering-students-pay-online-skill-education-syllabus-faculty-aicte-jobs-industry-gap-nep)

### Inferences
- **[INFERRED]** Planning funnel per active ambassador to get about 5 paid:
  1. Broadcast to class, department or hostel groups (100-300 students reached).
  2. About 40-80 show interest (click, form or demo attendance).
  3. About 15-25 have a 1:1 conversation.
  4. About 5 pay.
  - This implies **~8-12% of interested** and **~25% of 1:1 conversations** convert. Both are assumptions, to be validated in week 1-2.
- **[INFERRED] Price effects:**
  - At ₹999-₹2,999, impulse and peer purchase is feasible within days.
  - At ₹5,000-₹15,000, parents become involved and EMI or instalments are needed. Expect roughly half the conversion and a longer cycle, which the exam window squeezes.
  - No sourced EMI lift figure exists (gap carried from benchmarks_channels.md).
- **[INFERRED]** Group or "bring 3 friends" discounts suit hostel and class cohorts. No sourced lift figure exists.

### Gaps
- There is no published Indian student peer-referral conversion rate and no EMI-lift data.

## 4. Incentive design and fraud prevention

### Takeaway
Indian market practice for low-to-mid ticket courses has three common forms:
- a **flat ₹150-₹1,000 per paid enrollment**, with tier step-ups after about 5 sales
- **10-15% of sale value** in percentage schemes
- ₹5,000 per enrollment only for high-ticket programmes

Non-cash rewards (certificate, LoR, leaderboard prizes) are universal and are gated on a minimum performance bar. Payout only on confirmed, non-refunded payment is the main fraud and refund control.

| DigVerve for UDEN | 10% / 12% / 15% of sale (Bronze / Silver / Gold), plus referral-count bonuses | [SOURCED, JOB-LISTING] — [Weekday](https://jobs.weekday.works/campus-ambassador-at-digverve-4385672396) |

| Internshala (older) | ₹150 per training sale | [Internshala](https://internshala.com/blog/?p=15482) |

- Further programme features:
  - Edzy explicitly is *not* a referral-commission scheme. It is a structured, non-cash ambassador programme, a valid alternative model. [SOURCED, OPERATOR] — [Edzy](https://www.edzy.ai/student-ambassador)
  - Tiered rewards are recommended so that top performers earn premium perks. [SOURCED, VENDOR] — [Referral Rock](https://referralrock.com/blog/student-ambassador-programs/)

  - Internshala gates certificates on eligibility, pays monthly, and pays fixed fees for running seminars and fest stalls. [SOURCED, OPERATOR] — [Internshala blog](https://internshala.com/blog/?p=15482)

### Inferences
- **[INFERRED] Recommended structure for Tech Drona (₹2,000-₹5,000 course):**
  - **Commission:** 10-15% of net price (about ₹300-₹600) per paid enrollment, stepping up to 15-20% from the 6th sale.
  - **Bonuses:** Small fixed bonuses at 5, 10 and 20 sales. Leaderboard prizes for the top 3 per region.
  - **Activity fee:** A fixed amount (for example ₹500-₹1,000) for organising a TPO-approved workshop with 40+ attendees. This aligns ambassadors with the highest-converting channel.
  - **Certificate and LoR:** Certificate only at **3+ paid enrollments**. LoR at 10+. Internship-credit letter for the top tier.
- **[INFERRED] Fraud and leakage controls:**
  - Pay **only after the refund window closes** (for example 7 days) and on full payment, or on the instalment actually received.
  - Claw back on refund or chargeback.
  - Use unique codes or links per ambassador.
  - Do not pay commission on the ambassador's own purchase.
  - Verify each buyer with a college roll number or ID and an OTP-verified phone number distinct from the ambassador's.
  - Cap discount stacking.
  - Audit any ambassador with over 10 sales against attendance and logins.
  - Pay monthly or fortnightly, never late.

### Gaps
- No verified 2025-26 commission terms from major brands (Internshala current rate, Scaler, Coding Ninjas, PW Skills).
- No data on fraud rates in Indian ambassador programmes.

## 5. Realistic hours per week: exam season vs holidays

### Takeaway
The only Indian figure is Internshala's **2-3 hours/week**. In AP, 16 Nov to about 5 Dec 2026 is end-exam season for 2nd to 4th years. Plan for near-zero ambassador hours then and concentrate selling in 3-14 Nov and 29 Nov/6 Dec-31 Dec.

### Cited Findings
- Internshala ISP: 2-3 hours/week. [SOURCED, OPERATOR 2017] — [Internshala blog](https://internshala.com/blog/?p=15482)
- US paid-shift models assume about 10 hours/week, and that plan failed. Midterms caused roster loss and a reporting collapse by early November. [SOURCED, VENDOR] — [Makai capacity](https://www.makaiinc.com/resources/how-to-plan-student-ambassador-program-capacity-and-coverage); [Makai failure](https://www.makaiinc.com/resources/why-student-ambassador-programs-fail-a-field-diagnosis-and-recovery-guide)
- JNTUK exam dates are given in benchmarks_channels.md section 4:
  - 3rd/4th years: end exams 16-28 Nov
  - 2nd years: end exams 23 Nov-5 Dec
  - 1st years: end exams from 31 Dec
  - [SOURCED, UNOFFICIAL-MIRROR] — [jntufastupdates](https://www.jntufastupdates.com/jntuk-3-1-3-2-academic-calendar-2026-27/)

### Inferences
- **[INFERRED] Hours per week by phase:**

| Phase | Hours/week |
|---|---|
| Regular term | 3-5 |
| Mid exams (2-14 Nov, by year) | about 1-2 |
| End exams | 0-1 |
| Post-exam break and December | 5-8 |

- **[INFERRED]** The 8-week window has only about **5 effective selling weeks** per ambassador:
  - 3rd/4th-year ambassadors: about 9-14 Nov, then 29 Nov-31 Dec.
  - 2nd-year ambassadors: 3-11 Nov and 6-31 Dec.
  - 1st years: active Nov-mid-Dec, but they are less credible sellers and some are under 18, which raises a DPDP consent issue (see benchmarks_channels.md section 6).
- **[INFERRED]** Recruit ambassadors across year groups so that exam blocks are staggered. 2nd-year and 3rd-year ambassadors are the best mix.

### Gaps
- No Indian data on ambassador hours or output during exams.

## 6. Recommendation: per-ambassador targets, ambassadors per 10 enrollments, buffer and formula

### Takeaway
**[INFERRED, assumption-based]** Plan on about **2.5 paid enrollments per recruited ambassador**, which is about **5 per active ambassador** over the sprint.
- That means **4 recruited, or 2 active, ambassadors per 10 paid enrollments**.
- Reaching 400 from ambassadors alone takes about **160 recruited** (range 100-270).
- This sits at the top of the earlier 103-167 estimate. If workshops and other channels supply part of the 400, scale it down proportionally.

### Cited Findings
- Anchors used:
  - the affiliate tier distribution and active ratio — [Track360](https://track360.io/learn/affiliate-program-performance-benchmarking/partner-engagement-and-retention)
  - the 30-40% over-recruitment and 1-in-4 reserve rules, and the Tier 1 campus model of 4 active plus 2 reserve — [Makai](https://www.makaiinc.com/resources/how-to-plan-student-ambassador-program-capacity-and-coverage)
  - the 3-per-month minimum (IMAV), the 5-sales grand-prize bar (Internshala) and "inactive from day 1" (Internshala) — see section 1 and benchmarks_channels.md

### Inferences

**[INFERRED] Individual ambassador targets (8 weeks, about 5 effective weeks)**

| Level | Paid enrollments | Meaning |
|---|---|---|
| Minimum ("stay active") | **3** | Gate for the certificate; about 1 per effective fortnight |
| Standard target (average active) | **5-6** | Gate for the bonus tier |
| Stretch / star | **15-20+** | About 5% of recruits; earn LoR, top prizes and paid college-lead roles |

**[INFERRED] Productivity scenarios per recruited ambassador**

| Scenario | Paid per recruited | Active share | Recruited needed for 400 |
|---|---|---|---|
| Conservative (open sign-up, weak support) | 1.5 | ~30% | **~270** |
| Base (curated, college lead, weekly cadence) | 2.5 | ~50% | **~160** |
| Optimistic (curated, workshop-led, ₹<3k price) | 4.0 | ~60% | **~100** |

**[INFERRED] Formula per college**
- Let T_c = the paid-enrollment target for college c. Set it by size, for example in proportion to CSE/IT intake, with the sum of T_c equal to the ambassador-channel share of 400.
- Let p = paid enrollments per **active** ambassador (base 5; conservative 3; optimistic 7).
- Let a = expected active share (curated 0.5; mixed 0.4; open 0.3).
- **Ambassadors to recruit at college c: A_c = max(3, ceil(T_c / (p × a)))**
  - In the base case, p × a = 2.5, so A_c = ceil(T_c / 2.5).
  - The floor of 3 means a single dropout cannot zero out a college. It mirrors Makai's minimum two-person team plus a reserve.
- Add **1 paid college lead** per college: a top performer or a senior, on a higher commission override.
  - For example, 5-10% of college sales as an override, in the style of Collegechalo's 30% upline override but smaller.
  - The college lead runs the WhatsApp groups and the TPO relationship.
- **Buffer multiplier:** 1/a, which is **2.0x** for curated recruitment and **2.5-3.3x** for open forms. That is on top of the active ambassadors needed, not a 30% top-up.
- **Rolling backfill:** at the day-14 check (around 17 Nov) and again at the post-exam restart (around 1 Dec), replace anyone with zero activity. Pre-recruit a **25% reserve list** per college.

**Example (base case)**
- 30 colleges × about 13 paid each = 400.
- A_c = ceil(13 / 2.5) = **6 recruited per college** plus 1 lead.
- That gives **about 180 recruited plus 30 leads**.
- Expect about 90 active, and about 9 stars producing about a quarter to half of the sales.

**Per 10 paid enrollments**

| Basis | Ambassadors needed |
|---|---|
| Active, base | **2** |
| Recruited, curated | **4** |
| Recruited, open sign-up | **~6.5** |

**Instrumentation to replace these assumptions by week 3**
- Track per ambassador:
  - leads logged
  - 1:1 conversations
  - paid enrollments
  - days since last activity
- Recompute p and a weekly and re-size recruitment.

### Gaps
- Every productivity parameter (p, a, the distribution) is assumed. No Indian ed-tech publishes these. Tech Drona's first 2 weeks of data, 3-16 Nov before end exams, are the only way to calibrate them.
- These figures do not model the portion of the 400 that comes from direct workshops or digital channels rather than ambassadors.
